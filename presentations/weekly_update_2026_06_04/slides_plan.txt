slide 02  —  setup: where we started
============================================================
title: starting point — exp_049 baseline (Big-Math int-2000, Llama-3.2-3B)

paragraph 1:
exp_049 ran four candidates head-to-head with identical hyperparameters:
GRPO baseline, GRPO-S seq-level entropy, GTPO per-token confidence,
GTPO-EMA-flipped. Big-Math integer-answer subset (2000 examples, seed
3407), bs=1×ga=4×ng=4, 500 steps. all four trainers used a four-part
reward: format_exact + format_approximate + answer_exact + answer_numeric.

paragraph 2:
final reward L50: GRPO +5.36, GTPO-Conf +4.44, GTPO-EMA-flipped +3.39,
GRPO-S +0.60. none of the three shaped variants beat GRPO baseline.
inspection of the rewards showed shaped methods hit 0.62 num_maj (≥2 of 4
generations numerically correct) — better than baseline — but only 0.04-0.06
exact_top (≥2 in valid tag format). the per-token bonus seemed to land on
the format tags themselves, distorting the gradient that should teach the
model to wrap the right number in the correct tags.

slide 04  —  ablation: drop the answer-exact and format rewards
============================================================
title: ablation — what if we keep only the numeric-correctness reward?

paragraph 1:
exp_049 numonly axis: re-ran the same four methods on the same data
but with reward_funcs reduced to just reward_answer_numeric. removes
all tag/format pressure. tests whether the format reward was carrying
the methods to high total reward and whether shaping methods produce
correct numbers regardless of tags.

paragraph 2:
result: GRPO baseline collapses from +5.36 → +0.24 (22× drop). num_maj
falls from 0.54 to 0.24. the three shaped methods also collapse, but
the ranking inverts — GTPO-EMA-flipped becomes the best in this regime
(+0.273), GTPO-Conf becomes the worst (+0.138). KL signature is
striking: baseline drifts 17× more than shaped variants under sparse
reward (0.20 vs 0.012-0.015). the format reward is load-bearing, and
shaping acts as soft KL regularisation when reward is sparse.

slide 06  —  fix: mask the format tags out of the per-token bonus
============================================================
title: exp_050 — keep all rewards, mask shaping off format-tag tokens

paragraph 1:
hypothesis: per-token shaping was distorting gradients on format-control
tokens. those tokens (<start_working_out>, <end_working_out>, <SOLUTION>,
</SOLUTION>) are highly peaked — the model is very sure when emitting
them — so they collected outsized bonuses or penalties that drowned out
the format-reward signal. mask shaping off on tag positions: on those
positions the per-token shaped advantage is replaced by the seq-level
GRPO advantage; content tokens keep their shaped advantage.

paragraph 2:
the mask only changes the per-token methods (GTPO-Conf, GTPO-EMA-flipped).
GRPO has no shaping at all; GRPO-S shapes at the sequence level. result on
Big-Math int-2000 / Llama-3.2-3B: both per-token methods now beat the
GRPO baseline for the first time. GTPO-Conf +5.10 (was +4.44, Δ +0.40),
GTPO-EMA-flipped +5.16 (was +3.39, Δ +0.46). exact_top jumped 0.06→0.30
for GTPO-Conf and 0.04→0.30 for GTPO-EMA-flipped (5–7× improvement).
answer_exact reward flipped sign for both.

slide 08  —  port to Qwen3-4B
============================================================
title: exp_051 — does the win transfer to Qwen3-4B?

paragraph 1:
same code path as exp_050, only the base model changed
(Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct → Qwen/Qwen3-4B) and max_seq raised 2560→4096
to fit Qwen3's thinking-mode completions. dataset, methods, training
args identical. Qwen3 tokenizer probe confirmed the four format tags
encode to clean token-id subsequences in the new vocabulary.

paragraph 2:
mixed transfer. GRPO baseline finishes at +5.00 reward L50 with
exact_top 0.66 (Llama baseline was 0.18) — Qwen3 is much stronger at
math out-of-the-box and almost at ceiling. GTPO-Conf still wins
(Δ +0.31 vs baseline), but GTPO-EMA-flipped loses (Δ -0.67). KL is
5-10× smaller across all methods on Qwen3 — model barely moves during
RL. less room for shaping to differ from baseline in the near-ceiling
regime, and the more aggressive EMA bonus hurts where the milder
confidence bonus still helps.

slide 10  —  pick a harder subset to open room
============================================================
title: exp_052 — Big-Math integer ∩ Llama-8B solve_rate < 0.3

paragraph 1:
exp_051 hit a near-ceiling regime on Qwen3 — baseline exact_top 0.66
left little headroom for shaping to differentiate. Big-Math-RL-Verified
ships a `llama8b_solve_rate` field per problem; we used it to filter to
problems Llama-8B solves <30% of the time. integer-answer ∩ llama8b<0.3
gives 45,273 problems in the pool, sample 2000 with seed 3407.

paragraph 2:
baseline drops 5× as expected (+5.00 → +0.93 reward L50, exact_top
0.66 → 0.32). room opens. but mean completion length jumps to 2927 and
**52% of completions hit max_completion=3584** — Qwen3 thinking-mode
explodes on hard problems. method ranking inverts vs exp_051: GTPO-Conf
loses (Δ -0.23), GTPO-EMA-flipped wins (Δ +0.08). EMA smoothing seems
more robust to the noisy reward signal that clipping produces; the raw
per-token confidence bonus amplifies that noise. delta is tiny though.

slide 12  —  cross-experiment summary
============================================================
title: cross-experiment Δ vs GRPO baseline

paragraph 1:
no shaping wins universally. GTPO-Conf with tag-mask wins on both
"easy" regimes (Llama int-2000 Δ +0.40; Qwen3 int-2000 Δ +0.31) but
loses on hard Qwen3 (Δ -0.23). GTPO-EMA-flipped wins on Llama
(Δ +0.46), loses on easy Qwen3 (Δ -0.67), wins narrowly on hard
Qwen3 (Δ +0.08).

paragraph 2:
this is consistent with a story where format reward acts as
training-wheels: when the GRPO baseline is far from ceiling, format
reward gives a dense gradient signal that lifts the answer reward;
shaping helps refine. when baseline is at ceiling there's nothing to
refine. when reward is noisy (high clipping), EMA-smoothed shaping
beats the raw per-token bonus. the picture argues we should focus on
the regime where shaping is most useful — strong-but-not-ceiling
baselines on noisy signal.

slide 14  —  extra-hard subset + bigger compute (exp_053, RUNNING)
============================================================
title: exp_053 — Llama-8B solve_rate ≤ 1/8, ng=16, 1000 steps

paragraph 1:
exp_053 is the stress test of exp_052's gtpo_ema_flipped win. dataset
is filtered tighter: `llama8b_solve_rate < 0.125` (1/8) — 28,207 problems
in the pool, sample 8000 with seed 3407 (mean Llama-8B solve_rate
≈ 0.046, very hard). training budget is 4× the rollouts per prompt
(num_generations=16 vs 4) and 2× the steps (1000 vs 500). estimated
runtime ~36h per method on Qwen3-4B at max_seq=4096.

paragraph 2:
GRPO baseline finished cleanly at reward L50 **+5.54** with
exact_top 0.38 and **only 16% clipping** — counter-intuitively much
better than exp_052's grpo at +0.93 / 52% clip. ng=16 gives a much
cleaner advantage estimate per prompt (std=0 batches become rare),
so the model finds short correct tag-wrapped answers faster instead
of spiraling into thinking-mode max length. GTPO-EMA-flipped started
with the same 4-tag mask as before but showed 76% clipping in the
first 80 steps. observed root cause: Qwen3 emits `<think>...</think>`
natively, which the original mask did not cover; the EMA O- penalty
landed on the highly-peaked `</think>` close token and pushed the
model away from closing thinking. extended the mask to 6 tags
(4 ours + `<think>`, `</think>`), added 3 unit tests covering the
extended mask plus a static check that train.py actually wires all
6 tags, restarted gtpo_ema_flipped. now running; result pending.

slide 16  —  next: port methodology to Search-R1
============================================================
title: next dataset — Search-R1 (after Big-Math wraps)

paragraph 1:
once exp_053 lands and the Big-Math story is closed, the next
target is Search-R1 — an RL-for-tool-use benchmark where the model
issues `<search>...</search>` queries to a retrieval backend, reads
the snippet, and emits `<answer>...</answer>`. it has its own set
of structural tags (different from our `<SOLUTION>` / `<think>`)
and a sparser reward signal because the model only sees retrieved
context, not gold answers.

paragraph 2:
the tag-mask machinery in `src/format_tag_mask.py` is dataset-agnostic:
register the new tag substrings, the trainer pipeline keeps working.
hypothesis: Search-R1 likely lands in the regime where shaping helps —
stronger-than-trivial baseline on noisy tool-augmented reward, similar
to exp_052's clipping regime but cleaner. plan to start with the same
two-method pair (GRPO baseline + GTPO-EMA-flipped tag-masked) and the
ng=16 budget that worked on exp_053 grpo.
